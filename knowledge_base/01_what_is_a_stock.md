# What Is a Stock?

A stock represents a small ownership stake in a company. When you buy one share of a company's stock, you become a partial owner of that business, with a claim on a proportional slice of its assets and future earnings. Companies issue stock to raise money for growth — new products, expansion, hiring — without taking on debt.

## How stocks create value for an investor

There are two main ways a stockholder can benefit:

1. **Price appreciation** — if the company grows and becomes more valuable, the market price of its shares tends to rise, and an investor can sell at a higher price than they paid.
2. **Dividends** — some companies distribute a portion of their profits directly to shareholders on a regular schedule, typically quarterly. Not all companies pay dividends; younger, fast-growing companies often reinvest profits into the business instead.

## Common vs. preferred stock

Most individual investors hold **common stock**, which comes with voting rights on major company decisions (like electing the board of directors) but ranks behind other obligations if the company runs into financial trouble. **Preferred stock** typically has no voting rights but has a higher claim on assets and dividends than common stock, and dividend payments are usually fixed.

## Risk and volatility

Stock prices can move significantly in short periods based on company performance, industry trends, interest rates, and broader economic conditions. Because of this volatility, stocks are generally considered a higher-risk, higher-potential-return asset class compared to bonds or cash. Historically, stocks have delivered stronger long-term average returns than more conservative assets, but with no guarantee — an individual stock, or even the broader market, can lose value, sometimes substantially, over shorter time horizons.

## Key terms

- **Market capitalization**: the total value of a company's outstanding shares (share price × number of shares), often used to categorize companies as small-cap, mid-cap, or large-cap.
- **Shares outstanding**: the total number of shares a company has issued.
- **Ticker symbol**: the short letter code used to identify a stock on an exchange (e.g., AAPL for Apple).
