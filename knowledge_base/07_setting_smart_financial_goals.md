# Setting SMART Financial Goals

Vague financial intentions — "I want to save more" or "I want to retire comfortably" — are hard to act on. The SMART framework turns a vague intention into something concrete and trackable by making sure a goal is **Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound**.

## Breaking down each element

- **Specific**: define exactly what you're saving for — a house down payment, an emergency fund, retirement — rather than a general notion of "saving money."
- **Measurable**: attach a number. "Save $20,000" is measurable; "save a lot" is not.
- **Achievable**: the goal should be realistic given your income, expenses, and time frame — an achievable goal keeps motivation intact; a wildly unrealistic one often leads to giving up.
- **Relevant**: the goal should connect to what actually matters to you — competing goals (a house down payment vs. an emergency fund vs. retirement) may need to be prioritized or pursued in parallel with different weightings.
- **Time-bound**: set a target date. A deadline turns "someday" into a plan with a defined savings rate.

## From goal to a monthly number

Once a goal is specific, measurable, and time-bound, it can be converted into a required monthly savings rate. For example, saving $24,000 for a down payment in 4 years, with no assumed investment growth, requires $500/month. If that money is invested rather than left in cash, the required monthly amount can be lower, since investment growth contributes part of the total — this is where compound interest and goal planning intersect directly.

## Common categories of financial goals

- **Short-term (under 2 years)**: emergency fund, a vacation, a large purchase — typically kept in low-risk, easily accessible accounts since the time horizon doesn't allow much room to recover from a downturn.
- **Medium-term (2–10 years)**: a home down payment, a wedding, starting a business — often a blend of safety and moderate growth.
- **Long-term (10+ years)**: retirement, a child's education — generally has the most room for growth-oriented investments, given the long runway to ride out volatility.

## Revisiting goals over time

Financial goals aren't static. Income changes, priorities shift, and life events occur. Reviewing goals periodically — at least annually — and adjusting the required savings rate or timeline keeps a plan realistic rather than something set once and forgotten.

## Key terms

- **Savings rate**: the percentage of income set aside toward goals rather than spent.
- **Time horizon**: the length of time before the money is needed.
- **Emergency fund**: money set aside for unexpected expenses, typically kept in a highly liquid, low-risk account.
