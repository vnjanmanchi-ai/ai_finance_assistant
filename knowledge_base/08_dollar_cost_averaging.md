# Dollar-Cost Averaging Explained

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) is the practice of investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals — say, $500 every month — regardless of whether the market is up or down, rather than trying to invest a lump sum all at once at what seems like the "perfect" moment.

## How it works mechanically

Because the amount invested each period is fixed, you automatically buy more shares when prices are low and fewer shares when prices are high. Over many periods, this tends to average out the purchase price, reducing the impact of any single bad-timing decision.

## Why investors use this approach

1. **Removes the pressure of market timing**: consistently trying to predict short-term market movements is notoriously difficult, even for professional investors. DCA sidesteps that decision entirely by investing on a fixed schedule.
2. **Reduces emotional decision-making**: automatic, regular investing helps avoid the common pitfall of panic-selling during downturns or chasing gains after a rally.
3. **Fits naturally with regular income**: DCA aligns well with how most people actually save — a portion of each paycheck — rather than requiring a large lump sum upfront.

## What DCA doesn't do

Dollar-cost averaging does not guarantee a profit or protect fully against loss in a declining market — if prices trend downward over the entire investing period, DCA will not prevent a loss, though it may reduce the average cost basis compared to a poorly timed lump sum. Historical analysis also shows that, on average, investing a lump sum immediately tends to outperform DCA over long periods, simply because markets have historically trended upward over time and money invested sooner has more time to grow. DCA's main benefit is behavioral and risk-management, not a guaranteed higher return.

## When DCA is commonly used

- Automatic retirement account contributions (like a 401(k) deducted from every paycheck) are a natural, common form of DCA.
- Investors with a large lump sum (an inheritance, a bonus) who are uncomfortable investing it all at once sometimes use DCA to ease into the market gradually and reduce the emotional weight of a single timing decision.

## Key terms

- **Lump-sum investing**: investing all available money at once, rather than spreading it over time.
- **Cost basis**: the average price paid per share across multiple purchases.
- **Market timing**: attempting to buy and sell based on predictions of future price movements.
